# External Reviewer Recruitment Channels

Verified against public institutional pages on **2 August 2026**.

## Purpose

This shortlist identifies credible routes for finding real external reviewers. It does not appoint anyone and does not imply that an organisation or individual is available, suitable, independent or willing to participate.

Every proposed reviewer must still pass the project declaration, conflict and outcome-blinding checks.

## Recommended reviewer mix

Each case should normally receive:

1. one **methods reviewer** with experience in probability, statistics, reproducibility or research integrity; and
2. one **domain or evidence reviewer** with migration, border-policy, humanitarian or open-source evidence expertise.

For disputed cases, prefer reviewers with materially different professional perspectives. Do not use institutional diversity as a substitute for checking individual conflicts.

## First-wave channels

### University of Oxford — COMPAS and Migration Oxford

**Potential contribution:** migration research, migration-policy interpretation, forced displacement and mixed-cause explanations.

**Public routes:**

- https://www.compas.ox.ac.uk/people
- https://www.compas.ox.ac.uk/migration-oxford
- `info@compas.ox.ac.uk`
- `migration-mobility@torch.ox.ac.uk`

**Why approach:** COMPAS conducts migration research and Migration Oxford links migration scholars across the university, allowing a request to be circulated beyond one research team.

**Conflict checks:** exclude anyone who authored or materially contributed to a case source, advised a party to the dispute, or has already seen the frozen outcome packet.

### UK Reproducibility Network

**Potential contribution:** reproducibility, preregistration, transparent methods, auditability and leakage controls.

**Public routes:**

- https://www.ukrn.org/local-network-leads/
- https://www.ukrn.org/global-networks/
- `contact@ukrn.org`
- `lnl-chair@ukrn.org`

**Why approach:** UKRN is a peer-led network focused on rigorous and transparent research practices and provides institutional and local-network contact routes.

**Limit:** a reproducibility reviewer may not have migration-domain expertise and should normally be paired with a domain reviewer.

### Royal Statistical Society

**Potential contribution:** proper scoring, calibration, discrimination, statistical interpretation and professional review.

**Public routes:**

- https://rss.org.uk/about/contact-us/
- `info@rss.org.uk`
- `pa@rss.org.uk`
- https://rss.org.uk/membership/volunteering-and-promoting/current-volunteering-opportunities/

**Why approach:** the Society is a professional body and learned society with a large membership and established volunteering structures.

**Limit:** the Society is a recruitment channel, not evidence that any member is qualified for this specific method. Relevant statistical and review experience must be checked individually.

### Migration Policy Institute Europe

**Potential contribution:** European migration policy, asylum systems, policy mechanisms and institutional interpretation.

**Public routes:**

- https://www.migrationpolicy.org/mpi-europe/about-mpi-europe
- https://www.migrationpolicy.org/programs/mpi-europe/staff
- https://www.migrationpolicy.org/about/contact-and-directions

**Why approach:** MPI Europe describes itself as an independent nonprofit research institute working on migration and asylum policy in Europe.

**Conflict checks:** record policy-advisory relationships, prior work on a named case and authorship of any evidence or outcome source.

## Second-wave channels

### Mixed Migration Centre

**Potential contribution:** mixed migration, route dynamics, humanitarian drivers, field data and counter-explanations to state-coercion claims.

**Public routes:**

- https://mixedmigration.org/our-team/
- https://mixedmigration.org/contact-us/

**Why approach:** MMC conducts data collection, research and analysis on mixed migration through regional hubs.

**Conflict checks:** MMC is part of the Danish Refugee Council. Reviewers must disclose operational, donor, advocacy or case-specific involvement. Do not treat operational experience as neutral by default.

### Good Judgment

**Potential contribution:** probabilistic forecasting, scoring, calibration, question design and forecast-review practice.

**Public routes:**

- https://goodjudgment.com/contact/
- https://goodjudgment.com/services/

**Why approach:** Good Judgment provides professional forecasting and Superforecasting services and may be able to identify a methods reviewer.

**Cost and conflict note:** this may be a commercial route involving fees. No purchase or commitment is authorised. A reviewer affiliated with a forecasting provider must disclose commercial and methodological interests.

### UK Research Integrity Office

**Potential contribution:** advice on reviewer independence, conflict declarations, research conduct and integrity of the external-review process.

**Public routes:**

- https://ukrio.org/advice-services/
- https://ukrio.org/general-contact-information/
- `info@ukrio.org`

**Why approach:** UKRIO provides independent, confidential advice on research conduct and good practice across disciplines.

**Limit:** UKRIO may advise on process rather than supply reviewers. Its guidance is advisory and not a regulatory approval.

### OSINT Foundation

**Potential contribution:** open-source evidence provenance, source handling, reproducibility and intelligence-analysis practice.

**Public route:**

- https://www.osintfoundation.com/osint/Contact_Us.asp

**Conflict checks:** record government, defence, intelligence-community and contractor affiliations. Do not treat security-sector experience as automatic neutrality.

## Supplementary route

### Bellingcat community and training network

**Potential contribution:** open-source verification, transparent evidence trails and digital-investigation methods.

**Public routes:**

- https://www.bellingcat.com/about/who-we-are/
- https://www.bellingcat.com/workshops/

**Use cautiously:** approach only for source-provenance or verification expertise. Investigators may already know the historical outcome, have worked on related actors, or hold strong accountability positions. Outcome blinding and case-specific conflicts require close scrutiny.

## Recruitment order

1. Ask COMPAS or Migration Oxford to circulate the domain-review request.
2. Ask UKRN and the Royal Statistical Society for methods and reproducibility volunteers.
3. Approach MPI Europe for European policy expertise.
4. Use MMC for humanitarian and mixed-migration counter-perspectives where case conflicts permit.
5. Ask UKRIO for advice on the independence and declaration process.
6. Consider Good Judgment only if specialist forecasting review is needed and any cost is separately authorised.
7. Use OSINT Foundation or Bellingcat-linked routes only for evidence-provenance coverage and after security, advocacy and prior-exposure checks.

## Case allocation principles

### Positive institutional-attribution cases

Do not appoint someone who authored the later institutional outcome source. Pair a migration-policy reviewer with a methods or evidence-dependency reviewer.

### Negative or non-coercive cases

Include humanitarian or forced-displacement expertise, but avoid using an author of the decisive outcome evidence. Pair with a statistical or reproducibility reviewer.

### Disputed cases

Use reviewers from different professional perspectives. Neither reviewer should represent a state party or an institution that issued the frozen outcome position.

### Insufficient-public-information cases

Prioritise evidence-provenance, open-source limitations and command-inference expertise. Reviewers must understand that `insufficient` is a judgement about the public record, not proof of absence.
