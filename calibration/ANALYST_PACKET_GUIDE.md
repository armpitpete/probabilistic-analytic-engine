# Calibration Analyst Packet Guide

## Role separation

Each case requires:

- one person to prepare the evidence and probability assignment;
- a different person to approve the frozen inputs;
- at least two external reviewers who did neither.

Role separation reduces shared judgement risk. It does not guarantee independence or correctness.

## Outcome blinding

Analysts must work only with material dated on or before the case cutoff.

Do not seek or use:

- the frozen outcome class;
- the outcome rationale;
- withheld post-cutoff outcome sources;
- another case analyst's final probabilities;
- external reviewer findings from another case.

Accidental exposure must be recorded immediately. A contaminated case triggers the programme's stop or replacement-version process; it must not be silently repaired.

## Required work

1. Restate the question literally.
2. Check that all four outcome classes are meaningful for the question.
3. Verify each source date and location.
4. Record what each source is competent to establish.
5. Map republication, shared briefings and common evidence origins.
6. Search for contrary evidence and ordinary explanations.
7. Record inaccessible sources and unsuccessful searches.
8. State remaining critical information gaps.
9. Assign probabilities that total one.
10. Explain the prior and each material likelihood judgement.
11. Record sensitivity to assumptions and evidence removal.
12. State evidence confidence separately from probability.
13. Freeze the packet before approval.

## Four outcome classes

### Positive

Later public evidence supports deliberate state coercion under the case's declared resolution standard.

### Negative

Later public evidence supports a primarily non-coercive or ordinary mechanism under the declared standard.

### Disputed

Materially different competent accounts remain unresolved under the declared standard.

### Insufficient

The public record cannot satisfy the declared evidentiary question. This does not mean that no hidden conduct occurred.

## Probability discipline

- Do not assign a percentage merely because a source uses confident language.
- Do not count repeated reporting as independent corroboration.
- Do not infer command from motive, opportunity or temporal sequence alone.
- Do not force uncertainty into the disputed class when the real problem is insufficient public evidence.
- Do not use the historical corpus outcome balance as a prior for an individual case.
- Keep probability ranges and evidence confidence separate.

## Approval

The approver checks the exact frozen packet, not a verbal summary. Approval means that the inputs are coherent enough to score later. It does not mean agreement with the eventual historical outcome.
